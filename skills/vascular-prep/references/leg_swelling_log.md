# Leg Swelling & Edema Log Template

Tracking worksheet for patients with leg swelling, chronic venous insufficiency, or suspected lymphedema.

## Purpose
Leg swelling patterns (timing, symmetry, triggers, and skin changes) help your vascular specialist distinguish venous, lymphatic, cardiac, or medication-related causes and track response to compression or other therapies.

## How to Record
- Measure at the same time each day, ideally in the morning before getting up and in the evening before bed.
- Measure both legs at the same point (e.g., mid-calf and above the ankle) using a soft tape measure.
- Note symmetry: is one leg more swollen than the other?
- Note triggers: prolonged standing, sitting, travel, heat, salt intake, or menstrual cycle.
- Note skin changes: redness, darkening, thickening, warmth, tenderness, or new wounds.

## Weekly Summary
- Which leg is more swollen? Right / Left / Equal
- Time of day when swelling is worst: Morning / Afternoon / Evening
- Any new shortness of breath, chest pain, or rapid heartbeat alongside the swelling? Yes / No
- Any new wounds or non-healing sores on the foot or leg? Yes / No

## What to Bring to Your Visit
- The completed 14-day log.
- Photos of any skin changes, taken at the same angle and lighting.
- A list of any compression garments currently used, with class and hours worn per day.
