# Vascular Red Flag Warning Protocol

Clinical decision thresholds to distinguish routine vascular clinic discussion topics from life-threatening emergencies.

## Immediate 911 / Emergency Room Red Flags
Stop using this application and call 911 immediately if you experience:
1. **Acute Limb Ischemia**: Sudden severe pain in a leg, a limb that feels cold or looks pale or mottled, or sudden loss of movement or sensation in the leg.
2. **Acute Chest Discomfort with Limb Swelling**: Chest pain, shortness of breath, rapid heartbeat, or coughing up blood occurring alongside a swollen or painful leg (potential sign of pulmonary embolism from a DVT).
3. **Severe Acute Dyspnea**: Inability to breathe while resting or inability to speak more than a couple of words without pausing.
4. **Syncope**: Sudden unexplained loss of consciousness or fainting, particularly with leg pain or after prolonged immobility.
5. **Massive Bleeding**: Uncontrolled bleeding, vomiting blood, or black tarry stools while on anticoagulation for a prior clot.
6. **Stroke Signs (FAST)**: Sudden facial drooping, unilateral arm weakness, slurred speech, or acute loss of vision.

## Urgent Clinic Contact Signs (Call Physician Within 24 Hours)
Contact your vascular specialist's office or on-call nurse if you notice:
- New or rapidly worsening leg swelling, especially if asymmetric between legs.
- A red, warm, and tender area along a vein that is expanding or spreading.
- Skin changes around the ankle such as darkening, thickening, or a new open sore or ulcer.
- A wound on the foot or leg that is not healing, becoming more painful, or showing signs of infection.
- Claudication that is progressing rapidly, now occurring at rest, or waking you from sleep (rest pain).
- New calf or thigh pain after recent surgery, hospitalization, or prolonged travel, even without visible swelling.
