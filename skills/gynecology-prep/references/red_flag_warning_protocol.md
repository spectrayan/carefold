# Gynecology Red Flag Warning Protocol

Clinical decision thresholds to distinguish routine gynecological symptoms from acute or life-threatening emergencies.

## Immediate 911 / Emergency Room Red Flags
Stop using this application and call 911 immediately if you experience:
1. **Severe Hemorrhage**: Soaking through a pad or tampon every hour for more than 2 hours, passing large clots (larger than a golf ball), or feeling dizzy, faint, or short of breath.
2. **Acute Pelvic Pain with Fever**: Sudden severe pelvic pain accompanied by a fever, chills, or abnormal vaginal discharge (potential sign of pelvic inflammatory disease or abscess).
3. **Suspected Ectopic Pregnancy**: Severe one-sided pelvic or abdominal pain, especially with a positive pregnancy test, shoulder pain, or fainting.
4. **Ovarian Torsion Signs**: Sudden, severe, sharp one-sided pelvic pain with nausea and vomiting.
5. **Severe Allergic Reaction**: Swelling of the face, lips, tongue, or throat after starting a new medication or using a new product.
6. **Signs of Stroke (FAST)**: Sudden facial drooping, unilateral arm weakness, slurred speech, or acute loss of vision (especially with hormonal therapy).

## Urgent Clinic Contact Signs (Call Gynecologist Within 24 Hours)
Contact your gynecologist's office if you notice:
- New or worsening pelvic pain that is not relieved by over-the-counter pain relievers.
- Abnormal vaginal bleeding between periods or after menopause.
- A new breast lump or skin changes on the breast.
- Severe nausea and vomiting that prevents you from keeping fluids down.
- A positive pregnancy test with new spotting or cramping (confirm with your provider).
