# Men's Health Red Flag Warning Protocol

Clinical decision thresholds to distinguish routine urological symptoms from acute or life-threatening emergencies.

## Immediate 911 / Emergency Room Red Flags
Stop using this application and call 911 immediately if you experience:
1. **Acute Urinary Retention**: Complete inability to urinate, accompanied by severe lower abdominal/suprapubic pain and a distended bladder (a medical emergency requiring immediate catheterization).
2. **Testicular Torsion Signs**: Sudden, severe, sharp pain in one testicle, often with swelling, nausea, and vomiting. This is a time-critical surgical emergency (the testicle can be saved if treated within hours).
3. **Fournier's Gangrene / Severe Infection**: Rapidly worsening perineal or scrotal pain, redness, or swelling accompanied by high fever, rigors, or dizziness (a severe necrotizing bacterial infection requiring immediate surgery).
4. **Gross Hematuria with Clots / Syncope**: Passing large blood clots in the urine, or experiencing fainting/dizziness due to significant blood loss.
5. **Cardiovascular / Stroke Red Flags**: Chest pain, shortness of breath, or sudden facial drooping, arm weakness, or slurred speech.
6. **Severe Allergic Reaction**: Swelling of the face, lips, tongue, or throat after starting a new medication.

## Urgent Clinic Contact Signs (Call Urologist/PCP Within 24 Hours)
Contact your urologist's office or primary care provider if you notice:
- Blood in the urine (hematuria) that is new or worsening (without large clots or fainting).
- A new lump or swelling in the testicle or groin.
- Severe pain during urination (dysuria) that is not improving.
- A significant change in urinary habits (frequency, urgency, weak stream) that is worsening.
- A new or changing skin lesion on the penis or scrotum.
