# Allergy & Immunology Red Flag Warning Protocol

Clinical decision thresholds to distinguish routine allergic reactions from life-threatening emergencies.

## Immediate 911 / Emergency Room Red Flags
Stop using this application and call 911 immediately if you experience:
1. **Anaphylaxis Signs**: Swelling of the throat, tongue, or lips; difficulty breathing or swallowing; wheezing; dizziness or fainting; or a sudden drop in blood pressure (feeling very weak or confused).
2. **Severe Asthma Exacerbation**: Inability to speak in full sentences due to breathlessness, bluish lips or face, or using a rescue inhaler without relief.
3. **Widespread Severe Hives with Systemic Symptoms**: Hives covering a large part of the body accompanied by vomiting, abdominal pain, or a feeling of impending doom.
4. **Angioedema without Airway Compromise**: Rapid swelling of the face or lips, even if breathing is currently normal (requires urgent evaluation).
5. **Severe Medication Reaction**: Widespread rash with fever, blistering, or peeling skin after starting a new medication (e.g., SJS/TEN).

## Urgent Clinic Contact Signs (Call Allergist Within 24 Hours)
Contact your allergist's office or on-call physician if you notice:
- A new or worsening allergic reaction that does not respond to prescribed antihistamines.
- Increasing frequency or severity of asthma symptoms.
- A delayed reaction (e.g., 24-48 hours) at the site of a skin-prick test or immunotherapy injection.
- Swelling, redness, or pain at an immunotherapy injection site that is worsening.
