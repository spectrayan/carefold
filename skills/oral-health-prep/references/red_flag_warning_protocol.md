# Oral Health Red Flag Warning Protocol

Clinical decision thresholds to distinguish routine dental clinic discussion topics from acute or life-threatening emergencies.

## Immediate 911 / Emergency Room Red Flags
Stop using this application and call 911 immediately if you experience:
1. **Facial or Neck Swelling with Fever**: Rapidly spreading swelling of the face, jaw, or neck accompanied by fever, chills, or feeling very unwell (potential sign of a deep space infection or sepsis).
2. **Difficulty Swallowing or Breathing**: Trouble swallowing saliva, a muffled voice, drooling, or any difficulty breathing alongside oral or facial swelling (potential sign of airway compromise).
3. **Uncontrolled Bleeding After Extraction**: Bleeding from an extraction site that will not stop after 20-30 minutes of firm gauze pressure, especially while on anticoagulants.
4. **Severe Trismus with Systemic Symptoms**: Inability to open the mouth combined with fever, swelling, or difficulty swallowing.
5. **Trauma with Loss of Consciousness or Severe Pain**: Significant facial trauma, a knocked-out tooth with head injury, or uncontrolled pain after an accident.
6. **Stroke Signs (FAST)**: Sudden facial drooping, unilateral arm weakness, slurred speech, or acute loss of vision.

## Urgent Clinic Contact Signs (Call Dentist Within 24 Hours)
Contact your dentist's office or on-call line if you notice:
- A swollen, tender, or pus-filled area around a tooth or gum that is worsening.
- Severe tooth pain that wakes you from sleep or does not respond to over-the-counter pain relief.
- A broken or cracked tooth with sharp edges or exposed nerve.
- A dental abscess with fever but no difficulty swallowing or breathing.
- A dry socket after extraction (severe pain 2-3 days after extraction, bad taste, or visible bone).
- Persistent bleeding after extraction that is slowing but not stopping.
- New or worsening mouth sores that do not heal within 2 weeks.
