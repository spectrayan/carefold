# Metabolic Red Flag Warning Protocol

Clinical decision thresholds to distinguish routine glucose fluctuations from acute or life-threatening emergencies.

## Immediate 911 / Emergency Room Red Flags
Stop using this application and call 911 immediately if you experience:
1. **Severe Hypoglycemia**: Blood sugar below 54 mg/dL (3.0 mmol/L) accompanied by confusion, inability to eat or drink, seizures, or loss of consciousness.
2. **Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA) Signs**: High blood sugar (often >250 mg/dL), extreme thirst, frequent urination, nausea/vomiting, abdominal pain, confusion, and fruity-smelling breath.
3. **Hyperglycemic Hyperosmolar State (HHS) Signs**: Extremely high blood sugar (often >600 mg/dL), severe dehydration, confusion, lethargy, or seizures (more common in type 2 diabetes).
4. **Cardiovascular / Stroke Red Flags**: Chest pain, shortness of breath, or sudden facial drooping, arm weakness, or slurred speech.

## Urgent Clinic Contact Signs (Call Provider Within 24 Hours)
Contact your endocrinologist or primary care provider if you notice:
- Persistent blood sugar readings above your target range despite following your medication plan.
- Frequent or severe hypoglycemia episodes (blood sugar <70 mg/dL) that require assistance.
- New or worsening numbness, tingling, or pain in your feet (peripheral neuropathy).
- Unexplained weight loss or extreme fatigue.
- A new skin infection or wound on the foot that is slow to heal.
