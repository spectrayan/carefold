# Infectious Disease Red Flag Warning Protocol

Clinical decision thresholds to distinguish routine infections from acute or life-threatening emergencies.

## Immediate 911 / Emergency Room Red Flags
Stop using this application and call 911 immediately if you experience:
1. **Sepsis Signs**: High fever or very low temperature combined with confusion, rapid breathing, rapid heart rate, or extreme weakness.
2. **Meningitis Signs**: Sudden high fever with a severe headache, stiff neck, sensitivity to light, or a new non-blanching rash (purple spots that do not fade when pressed).
3. **Severe Respiratory Distress**: Inability to breathe, severe shortness of breath at rest, or bluish lips/face.
4. **Severe Dehydration**: Inability to keep fluids down for more than 24 hours, no urination, dizziness, or fainting.
5. **Hemorrhagic Signs**: Unexplained bleeding from the gums, nose, or under the skin, especially after travel to a tropical region.
6. **Altered Mental Status**: Confusion, seizures, or extreme lethargy in the context of an infection.

## Urgent Clinic Contact Signs (Call Physician Within 24 Hours)
Contact your infectious disease specialist or primary care physician if you notice:
- A persistent fever lasting more than 3-5 days without improvement.
- A new rash, especially if it is spreading or accompanied by fever.
- Worsening symptoms despite being on antibiotics for 48-72 hours.
- Severe diarrhea or vomiting that is not improving.
- A new insect bite with an expanding red ring or flu-like symptoms.
- Swelling, redness, or pain at an IV site or wound.
